## Abstract 

Multimodal graph recommendation integrates observed interac-tions with item images and text to learn user preferences. These in-puts constitute Semantic–Behavioral Evidence (SBE). Many existing graph methods incorporate collaborative and multimodal semantic relations, yet their aggregation and state updates remain context-separated, limiting the continuous composition of preference evi-dence across layers and the joint use of multiple sources. To address this limitation, we propose CC-MGRec, a context-coupled graph learning method that organizes SBE within a common graph learn-ing context for cross-relation modeling and compositional propaga-tion. Its context-coupled multimodal graph integrates user–item col-laborative relations and item–item semantic relations, coordinating their message scales through joint normalization. Recommendation-grounded semantic calibration uses training behavior to adjust se-mantic neighbor weights while preserving normalized collaborative propagation coefficients. Collaborative–semantic compositional propagation updates shared node states layer by layer to contin-uously compose collaborative and multimodal semantic evidence, implicitly forming Preference Evidence Chains (PECs), including collaborative–semantic and visual–textual PECs. Joint execution of relation messages on the same shared node states within each layer preserves propagation outputs while reducing intermediate output processing and fusion overhead. We provide theoretical anal-ysis of the proposed mechanisms. Experiments on three Amazon datasets demonstrate improved recommendation quality and faster inference during graph propagation.

VBPR and ACF incorporate content into preference representations [3, 9]. ## 2.2 Graph Learning for Recommendation 

Graph learning organizes evidence as nodes and relations, model-ing higher-order associations through neighborhood propagation [27]. Table 1 compares representative methods across evidence expression, graph construction and propagation, and PEC support. EgoGCN organizes multimodal messages through edge-wise modulation, while GRCN refines observed interaction relations [2, 29]. LATTICE, MICRO, FREEDOM, and MGCN introduce item semantic relations [35, 39–41]; LGMRec and DOGE further incor-porate higher-order structures [7, 21]. In rating-feedback settings, SiMGR distinguishes positive and negative interaction relations [18]. Propagation organization determines how evidence carried by different relations participates in state updates. MMGCN and MIG-GT use modality-specific propagation [11, 30], whereas CRANE, MGCN, and DHMRec connect collaborative and semantic learning through staged updates [4, 35, 37]. UGT unifies multimodal feature learning on the interaction graph [34], while SCALE learns col-laborative and semantic representations separately and connects them through alignment and fusion [15]. These studies organize evidence through different graph construction and propagation choices. From the perspective of PECs, existing structures support different forms of evidence composition. MGCN and DHMRec sup-port C–S PECs through cross-relation state transfer [35, 37], while the merged semantic relations in LATTICE and FREEDOM support V–T PECs at sufficient propagation depth [39, 41]. Graph learning research also addresses propagation efficiency [1]. HAGs reduce redundant computation through aggregation reuse [13], while Hec-tor and TLV-HGNN optimize relational graph computation [8, 31]. Our CC-MGRec organizes multimodal content, observed interac-tions, and behavioral co-occurrence information within a common graph learning context to support continuous evidence composition across relations and efficient inference.

## 4 Methodology 4.1 Framework Overview 

CC-MG is the core of CC-MGRec: it organizes multimodal node information and weighted collaborative and semantic relations in a shared graph learning context (Figure 3). RGSC refines se-mantic neighbor weights using training behavior while preserving their incoming budgets. CC-MG combines these calibrated con-nections with semantic node anchors and weighted interactions, then jointly normalizes the resulting graph. CSCP learns from this graph through one evolving node state, enabling cross-relation composition in a single propagation stream. We first introduce the calibration rule used by CC-MG, followed by graph construction, propagation, and recommendation learning.
