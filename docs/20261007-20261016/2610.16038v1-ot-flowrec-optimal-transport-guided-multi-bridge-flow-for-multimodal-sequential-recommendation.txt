# ABSTRACT 

E-commerce user preferences evolve dynamically within sessions, exhibiting con-current and multi-centric interest distributions. While existing multimodal se-quential recommendation methods leverage global attention to compress histori-cal interactions, they inadequately capture the concurrent nature of multi-interest behaviors and fail to model the underlying multi-centric distribution of user in-tent. To tackle this issue, we propose OT-FlowRec, an optimal transport-guided multi-bridge flow matching framework that explicitly models multi-interest dis-tributions to facilitate multimodal sequential recommendation. Unlike single-path generative models, OT-FlowRec constructs a mixture of parallel, interest-specific transport flows to model the multi-centric distribution characteristics, where each flow preserves a distinct user interest cluster via an optimal transport plan. Then, a geometric transport mechanism is designed to establish minimum-cost paths from the latent multi-interest distribution to target preference prototypes to pro-vide multi-interest awareness. Moreover, a progressive time-sampling warmup strategy dynamically biases optimization weights toward the final states of the generation process, bridging the gap between continuous global distribution fit-ting and practical top-k ranking metrics. This approach achieves explicit interest disentanglement, ensuring the preservation of a multi-peaked preference represen-tation instead of collapsing it into a unimodal average. Extensive experiments on three public datasets demonstrate that OT-FlowRec outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Multimodal sequential recommendation (MSR) has emerged as a pivotal paradigm for delivering personalized services in e-commerce, driven by its capacity to model the temporal dynamics of user preferences. By synergistically integrating interaction sequences with complementary signals derived from text and images, MSR is advantageous in disambiguating items that share similar behavioral footprints yet differ semantically, meanwhile enriching preference inference from sparse or inherently noisy user interactions. This enables MSR to capture users’ nuanced, often concurrent interests—a capability that pure sequential models lack, and has drawn growing research attention. (Pan et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a; Ji et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024c; Xu et al., 2025; Xv et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a; Liu et al., 2024b; Fang et al., 2024). A substantial body of research has been devoted to MSR, which can be categorized into three techni-cal lines: traditional sequential methods, diffusion-based methods, and flow-based methods. Specif-ically, traditional sequential methods use recurrent networks or self-attention to capture ordered dependencies through discriminative learning (Hidasi et al., 2016; Kang & McAuley, 2018; Sun et al., 2019). Diffusion-based methods learn stochastic denoising processes in continuous spaces, improving robustness to noisy interactions and supporting flexible generation of target-item repre-sentations (Xie et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025c). Flow-based methods employ vector-field learning and deterministic ODE evolution, reducing the stochasticity and itera-tive cost of diffusion sampling while directly modeling continuous preference trajectories (Liu et al., 2025b;a; Huang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b). 1arXiv:2610.16038v1 [cs.IR] 12 Oct 2026 Figure 1: User interaction paths in the Toys dataset. Colors denote latent interest categories; this shows that multiple interests coexist within a session. Although promising success has been achieved by previous works, two primary limitations remain. (i) Multi-centric in-terest distribution collapse. Existing global attention-based approaches com-press multi-peaked interactions into sin-gle deterministic embeddings, which fail to describe the intrinsic multi-centric dis-tribution characteristics. Moreover, most existing diffusion-based and flow-based methods construct single-peaked distribu-tion representation, which is insufficient to model the multi-centric distribution of user intent. (Kang & McAuley, 2018; Sun et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2025b) (Figure 1). (ii) Static transport with misaligned priors. Prevailing flow-based generative recom-menders construct time-dependent conditional paths based on independently sampled prior distribu-tions, without accounting for the underlying optimal transport geometry between users’ historical interactions and their target preferences. Consequently, the learned trajectories are directionally un-constrained and lack semantic grounding to the multi-interest prototypes (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025a). To address these limitations, we propose OT-FlowRec, an optimal transport-guided multi-bridge flow matching framework. Rather than collapsing a complex interaction sequence into a single representation, a Multi-Prototype Guided Source Distribution (MPSD) module is constructed to represent concurrent user preferences with multi-interest source distribution. Its interest-specific components initialize parallel transport flows, preserving the underlying multi-peaked structure and avoiding unimodal collapse. Considering the advantage of interest prototypes (Wang et al., 2023a) in capturing diverse user semantics, an OT-Guided Multi-Bridge Flow Matching (OT-MBFM) module is further built, where multiple preference prototypes are first constructed. Accordingly, an interest distribution-to-prototype transformation learning process is then designed to map the multiple in-terest distribution to their matched prototypes through those parallel transport flows. To facilitate the transformation, minimum-cost paths are computed to connect the latent source distribution to target preference prototypes, ensuring explicit awareness of diverse user interests during generation. Furthermore, a mismatch remains between continuous distribution fitting and discrete ranking eval-uation during the sampling process. We therefore develop a Progressive Time-sampling Warmup (PTW) that progressively shifts optimization toward the terminal states of the generation trajectory. By focusing training on these later states, PTW narrows the gap between continuous trajectory fitting and top-k ranking. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose OT-FlowRec, a multi-bridge flow learning framework that explicitly encodes the intrinsic multi-centric distribution of user interests. Unlike global attention mechanisms that compress concurrent interests into single deterministic embeddings, OT-FlowRec con-structs a mixture of parallel, interest-specific transport flows, achieving effective multi-interest disentanglement while avoiding the multi-centric interest distribution collapse com-monly observed in global attention methods. • We propose the OT-MBFM module which establishes minimum-cost paths from the la-tent multi-interest distribution to target preference prototypes, providing directionally con-strained trajectories. Specifically, a PTW strategy is designed to improve ranking quality by dynamically shifting optimization weights towards the terminal generative states, which effectively aligns continuous distribution fitting with top-k ranking metrics. • Extensive experiments on three public datasets demonstrate that our framework outper-forms existing state-of-the-art baselines across all evaluation metrics. 2Figure 2: The overall architecture of OT-FlowRec. RQ2: We explore how the key modules of OT-FlowRec, including MPSD, OT-MBFM, and PTW, contribute to the overall recommendation performance, and we analyze the specific roles they play within the model. RQ3: We examine how the proposed MPSD, OT-MBFM, and PTW modules improve recommen-dation performance and generation quality compared with traditional generative recommendation models that rely on single global representations and standard flow matching. 3.1.1 DATASETS AND EVALUATION METRICS

To evaluate OT-FlowRec, we compare it against three categories of baseline methods: (1) Tradi-tional sequential models , including RNN-based and Transformer-based architectures (GRU4Rec (Hidasi et al., 2016), SASRec (Kang & McAuley, 2018), BERT4Rec (Sun et al., 2019)) and those integrating side information (MSSR (Lin et al., 2024)); (2) Diffusion-based models , spanning foun-dational frameworks (DreamRec (Yang et al., 2023), DiffuRec (Li et al., 2023)) and advanced vari-ants (DiffMM (Jiang et al., 2024), ADRec (Chen et al., 2025), MoDiCF (Li et al., 2025a), PreferDiff (Liu et al., 2025c)); and (3) Flow-based models tailored for recommendation (FMRec (Liu et al., 2025b), FlowCF (Liu et al., 2025a), FindRec (Wang et al., 2025)). 3.1.3 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

We implement the proposed method using the widely adopted RecBole (Zhao et al., 2021) frame-work. For all datasets, we filter out users and items with fewer than 5 interactions and truncate 6historical sequences to a maximum length of 50. The models are trained for up to 300 epochs us-ing the Adam optimizer, with a training batch size of 1024 and an evaluation batch size of 4096. We apply an early-stopping strategy if validation performance does not improve for 5 consecutive evaluations. Performance is evaluated using Hit and NDCG at K ∈ { 10 , 20 }. Textual and visual features are extracted using BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) (768 dimensions) and CLIP-ViT (Radford et al., 2021) (512 dimensions), respectively, which are then mapped to the hidden dimension d via linear layers. # 4 RELATED WORK 

4.1 TRADITIONAL SEQUENTIAL RECOMMENDATION 

Traditional sequential recommendation methods primarily learn behavior transition patterns from user historical interaction sequences (Cen et al., 2020; Li et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2022; Qin et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Tan et al., 2021; Pan et al., 2026; 8Park et al., 2025; Duan et al., 2025; Hou et al., 2022). Early approaches utilized Markov chains, RNNs (Hidasi et al., 2016), and CNNs (Tang & Wang, 2018), which were later broadly superseded by Transformer-based architectures like SASRec (Kang & McAuley, 2018) and BERT4Rec (Sun et al., 2019) to better capture sequence dependencies. To alleviate interaction sparsity, subsequent research incorporated auxiliary multimodal information (e.g., text and images) (Hu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a; Kim et al., 2025; Ji et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024c; Xu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Xv et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a; Liu et al., 2024b; Fang et al., 2024), utilizing techniques like multi-sequence attention (Lin et al., 2024) and modality experts (Guo et al., 2025). However, these methods typically remain within the discriminative modeling paradigm, compressing complex user behaviors into deterministic, single-point representations. This limitation prevents them from fully capturing the multi-peaked distributional nature and generative evolution of dynamic user interests. 4.2 DIFFUSION -B ASED RECOMMENDATION 

Diffusion-based methods shift recommendation from discriminative matching to generative distri-bution modeling (Xie et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025a; Qu & Nobuhara, 2025; Mao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025c; Wang et al., 2023b). Early works like DiffuRec (Li et al., 2023) and DreamRec (Yang et al., 2023) reconstruct target item representations via stochastic noise-addition and reverse denoising processes, thereby capturing diverse user interests as continuous distributions rather than fixed vectors. Subsequent research has enhanced this paradigm by integrating multimodal user-item graphs (Jiang et al., 2024), aligning generation with personalized ranking objectives (Liu et al., 2025c), and em-ploying token-level diffusion to prevent embedding collapse (Chen et al., 2025). Despite these advancements, the inherent reliance on curved probability paths and multi-step stochastic sampling limits their inference efficiency and often leads to error accumulation during the generative process. 4.3 FLOW -B ASED RECOMMENDATION 

Flow-based models replace the stochastic denoising of diffusion models with deterministic contin-uous generation, transporting prior distributions to target distributions via time-dependent vector fields (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024). Recent works have adapted flow matching for rec-ommendation by designing discrete flows for implicit feedback (Liu et al., 2025a), utilizing straight trajectories to reduce preference perturbations (Liu et al., 2025b), and employing Stein-guided en-tropic flows to capture multi-peaked sequential preferences (Wang et al., 2025). Despite improving inference efficiency and stability over diffusion models, existing flow match-ing methods typically construct simple, global straight paths. This ignores the explicit geometric transport costs between historical distributions and latent multi-interest prototypes. Furthermore, their continuous distribution fitting objectives often misalign with discrete top-k ranking metrics. To bridge these gaps, our OT-FlowRec proposes OT-MBFM for explicit interest disentanglement, alongside the PTW strategy to directly optimize late-stage generation for practical ranking accuracy.

In this paper, we proposed OT-FlowRec to jointly model concurrent user interests in multimodal sequential recommendation. To overcome unimodal collapse, our framework constructed parallel transport flows that disentangled diverse interests and preserved multi-peaked preference distri-butions. Integrating minimum-cost geometric transport paths with the progressive time-sampling warmup strategy achieved explicit multi-interest awareness and bridged continuous distribution fit-ting and discrete top-k ranking metrics. Validation curves corroborate these conclusions from the perspective of optimization dynamics. As Figure 6 illustrates, the full model achieves more stable and higher validation N@20 scores across the Beauty, Video Games, and Toys datasets. Although per-formance gaps among variants are smaller on the Beauty dataset, the full model still performs best. This suggests that the marginal benefits of individual modules diminish when user interest structures are smooth, whereas the advantages of the optimal-transport-guided multi-bridge flow mechanism become more prominent on datasets with diverse interests and complex semantics. The training curve for the w/o PTW variant highlights the core contribution of PTW in aligning continuous distribution fitting with practical ranking metrics. By progressively adjusting the beta distribution bias for time-step sampling, PTW dynamically biases optimization weights toward the final states of the generation process, rather than relying on balanced full-path supervision. During early training stages, this variant still exhibits an upward trend comparable to the full model, sug-gesting that the basic optimal-transport bridge flow alone can learn effective directions for interest evolution. In later stages, however, it frequently plateaus or oscillates, failing to reliably achieve the optimal validation N@20 of the full model. This performance gap is especially evident on the Video Games and Toys datasets, where the full model maintains a distinct lead. These findings confirm that without dynamically biasing weights toward the final states of the evolution process, the model captures the overall transport path but struggles to refine the local discriminative capability required for practical top-k ranking.
