## Abstract 

Graph Collaborative Filtering models have achieved remarkable success in recom-mendation systems. However, they inherently suffer from a catastrophic repre-sentation collapse in strict cold-start scenarios due to the topological isolation of unobserved items. While integrating multi-modal semantic features offers a poten-tial remedy, directly mapping isolated semantic attributes into a globally topology-driven collaborative space frequently precipitates representation degradation and optimization conflicts. To bridge this fundamental structural divide, we propose DualGCN, an innovative Dual-Branch Graph Knowledge Distillation Framework. Operating under a decoupled two-stage training paradigm, DualGCN first constructs a robust collaborative teacher network where item representations are anchored by a topology-smoothed semantic prior, effectively reshaping the latent manifold. Sub-sequently, a semantic student branch employs a residual mapping mechanism and a magnitude-agnostic cosine distillation objective to achieve high-fidelity cross-modal alignment without disrupting established collaborative signals. Furthermore, we introduce a dynamic norm reconstruction mechanism to rigorously calibrate the physical scales of novel and warm items during global retrieval. Extensive ex-periments across three real-world benchmark datasets demonstrate that DualGCN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in strict cold-start scenarios while comprehensively preserving recommendation accuracy for highly active items. iarXiv:2610.19578v1 [cs.IR] 14 Oct 2026 1 Introduction 

Recommender systems are core components of modern online platforms, including e-commerce and social media [1–4]. Among existing paradigms, Collaborative Filtering (CF) remains fundamental due to its domain-agnostic nature and proficiency in capturing user in-terests from historical interactions [5–8]. Because these interactions naturally form bipartite graphs, Graph Collaborative Filtering (GCF) has emerged as a dominant approach [9–11]. Specifically, ID-based GCF models explicitly capture high-order collaborative signals via multi-hop message passing [12]. Architectures such as LightGCN [13] and LR-GCCF [14] further streamline this paradigm by omitting non-linear transformations, achieving high training and inference efficiency through a minimalist representation space. However, ID-only models inherently struggle with extreme data sparsity and the cold-start problem. Without sufficient interaction history, cold-start items cannot be effectively mapped into the mature collaborative space. This creates a severe distribution shift between warm and cold items, significantly bottlenecking model performance [15, 16]. To mitigate this, a common practice is to incorporate side semantic features (e.g., text or multimodal metadata) to enrich the representation space [17–23]. While providing valuable contextual priors to bridge sparse interaction chains [21, 24–26], these hybrid models typically rely on complex feature extractors and multi-layer fusion modules. This complexity not only escalates computational overhead but often compromises the purity of collaborative signals, thereby degrading general recommendation performance [27, 28]. This dilemma raises a critical question: Can we leverage the rich priors of semantic features for cold-start items while preserving the lightweight architecture and collaborative efficiency of ID-based models? 

To answer this, we propose DualGCN, a novel dual-path graph knowledge distillation frame-work for cold-start recommendation. DualGCN enables approximate topological-to-semantic knowledge transfer without relying on heavy fusion modules. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We introduce DualGCN, a lightweight distillation framework featuring an asymmetric dual-path architecture. By replacing traditional heavy fusion modules with this design, it achieves approximate semanticknowledge transfer to the topological space, drastically reducing online inference costs. 1• We design a two-stage optimization paradigm that decouples topological updates from semantic alignment, effectively resolving gradient conflicts common in multitask graph training. Coupled with a novel topology-smoothed semantic prior, the model successfully aligns cold-start nodes within the collaborative space. 

• Extensive experiments on multiple real-world datasets demonstrate that DualGCN signif-icantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in graph collaborative recommendation and achieves superior performance in cold-start scenarios. 

# 2 Related Work 

## 2.1 Graph-based Collaborative Filtering 

Conventional Matrix Factorization (MF) models [29] primarily capture first-order interac-tions between users and items, often overlooking the intricate topological dependencies inherent in user-item data. To address this limitation, NGCF [12] introduced a message-passing mecha-nism via Graph Neural Networks to explicitly model high-order collaborative signals. Building on this, [13] proposed a streamlined architecture by removing redundant feature transformations and non-linear activations. This simplification not only reduced computational complexity but also established LightGCN as a state-of-the-art benchmark in graph-based recommendation. However, these models remain heavily reliant on the connectivity of the interaction bipartite graph. In scenarios characterized by long-tail items or extreme data sparsity, the high-order infor-mation propagation mechanism often falters [16], which fundamentally constrains the model’s representation capability. 

## 2.2 Cold-start Recommendation 

To compensate for the scarcity of interaction signals, researchers have extensively explored leveraging item metadata, such as textual descriptions, categories, or multimodal attributes, to enhance recommendation performance. Current cold-start solutions generally bifurcate into approach-driven and data-driven strategies. Approach-driven strategies, such as Dropout-Net [18], explicitly optimize cold-start performance by randomly dropping collaborative features during training. Meta-learning models, such as MetaEmbedding [19], utilize an embedding gen-erator to produce high-quality initial ID embeddings for new items based on their side information 2and a limited set of interactions. Conversely, data-driven strategies focus on augmenting the interaction matrix. For instance, content-boosted models [30] utilize content features to gen-erate pseudo-ratings for sparse user-item pairs before applying traditional CF. DeepMusic [17] pioneered the use of deep neural networks to extract textual and acoustic features from side attributes and map them into the CF latent space, demonstrating that integrating content features effectively mitigates the cold-start dilemma. Furthermore, weakly supervised methods [31] leverage user content preferences and item attributes to score new items, bridging the gap left by missing interactions. 

## 2.3 Representation Alignment in Recommendation 

While content features provide a viable path to alleviate cold-start issues, an inherent hetero-geneous gap exists between the topology-based ID space and the attribute-based semantic space. To bridge this discrepancy, frontier research often relies on complex external graph structures for cross-view alignment. For example, DCIB [32] employs a dual contrastive information bottleneck to facilitate topological knowledge transfer from knowledge graphs to collaborative views. Similarly, DFHE [33] injects structural information into the semantic view by fusing meta-graphs and meta-paths. However, these methods depend heavily on complete prior knowl-edge bases, such as high-quality knowledge graphs or predefined heterogeneous information networks. In many practical and stringent industrial scenarios, such external knowledge is often unavailable [34–36], leaving the model to rely solely on highly sparse bipartite graphs and mini-mal item features. Consequently, achieving efficient cross-space representation alignment, while decoupling the model from external graph structures and relying only on minimalist features, remains a critical and unresolved challenge. 

# 3 Preliminaries 

In this chapter, we first formalize the candidate retrieval and strict cold-start problems in recommender systems, alongside a brief review of the graph convolutional paradigm exemplified by LightGCN. Subsequently, we analyze the fundamental causes of GCN failure in cold-start scenarios from a message-passing perspective. Finally, through preliminary empirical analysis, we demonstrate the efficacy of substituting random ID embeddings with pre-trained seman-tic features, providing both theoretical and empirical motivation for the proposed dual-path 3architecture.

## 4.2 Decoupled Two-stage Optimization 

Graph neural networks inevitably suffer from the over-smoothing effect during message passing. If topological updates and semantic alignment are jointly optimized in an end-to-end framework, the dominant gradients from the highly sparse graph reconstruction can easily over-whelm the low-frequency alignment signals, thereby disrupting the inherent representations of the static semantic space. To isolate this gradient interference and ensure that the original rec-ommendation accuracy for warm items is not compromised by the mapping network, DualGCN adopts a decoupled two-stage training paradigm. The primary objective of the first stage is to construct a robust collaborative reference frame. During this phase, the parameters of the semantic alignment network {W1, W2, b1, b2} are strictly frozen. The semantic features serve merely as structural anchors and do not participate 12 in the gradient updates of the learnable embeddings. ## 5.1 Experimental Settings 

This section delineates the experimental setup, encompassing dataset characteristics, data preprocessing and partitioning strategies, evaluation metrics, baseline models, and specific hyperparameter configurations. 

5.1.1 Datasets 

We conduct extensive experiments on the widely recognized Amazon Reviews’23 dataset [41], which serves as a standard benchmark in the recommendation domain by providing both rich multimodal textual attributes and real-world interaction records. Specifically, we select three domain-specific subsets: Amazon-Books, Amazon-Electronics, and Amazon-Sports. To ensure evaluation reliability and maintain consistency with standard protocols in GCF research [12,13], we apply a rigorous 10-core filtering strategy. This process eliminates users and items with fewer than 10 global interactions, thereby guaranteeing a sufficient baseline of collaborative signals 16 within the initial feature space. 5.1.2 Data Preprocessing and Splitting 

In the feature extraction phase, we leverage the all-mpnet-base-v2 [37] encoder to map the composite textual descriptions of items, specifically their titles and categories, into 768-dimensional dense vectors. A subsequent PCA projects these high-dimensional representations into a compact 64-dimensional latent space. To establish a rigorous data partitioning scheme, we randomly isolate 10% of the items to form the strict cold-start group. The interaction records of this subset are completely masked during the training phase to preclude any potential data leakage. The remaining 90% of items constitute the warm-start group, and their historical interactions are randomly partitioned into training, validation, and test sets according to a 7:1:2 ratio. 

5.1.3 Evaluation Metrics 

To rigorously evaluate the ranking performance in conventional warm-start scenarios, we adopt a full-ranking strategy. For each user in the test set, the model computes preference scores and globally ranks the ground-truth interacted items against all unobserved items. Conversely, when assessing out-of-distribution cold-start performance, it is crucial to eliminate the con-founding effect of popularity bias from trending items. Therefore, the candidate pool for the full ranking evaluation is strictly restricted to the pure cold-start item set. We employ Recall@ 𝐾 and NDCG@ 𝐾 as our primary evaluation metrics. The final reported performance results are the average scores across all users in the test set. To ensure alignment with the baseline models, the truncation length is configured to 𝐾 ∈ { 20 , 50 }.

5.1.4 Baselines 

To validate the superiority and efficacy of our proposed framework, we benchmark DualGCN against two representative categories of baseline methods: pure ID-based collaborative filtering 17 models and feature-augmented cold-start models. For a fair and rigorous comparison, the semantic inputs for all feature-driven baselines are standardized to the identical 64-dimensional vectors extracted from item titles and categories. 

Pure ID-based Collaborative Filtering 

• MF [29]: As the foundational baseline for collaborative filtering, Matrix Factorization projects users and items into low-dimensional latent vectors. It relies exclusively on direct interaction histories and is optimized via the Bayesian Personalized Ranking objective. 

• NGCF [12]: This graph-based model enriches representation learning by executing em-bedding propagation over the bipartite interaction graph. Such a mechanism explicitly captures the high-order connectivity inherent in the topological structure. 

• LightGCN [13]: By innovatively stripping away the redundant non-linear activations and feature transformations prevalent in traditional graph convolutional networks, this architecture drastically enhances both training efficiency and generalization capability. It currently stands as the state-of-the-art benchmark for pure identity-driven graph collabo-rative filtering. 

Feature-based and Cold-start Models 

• DeepMusic [17]: This approach employs deep neural networks to extract heterogeneous modal features, such as textual and acoustic attributes, from side metadata. These extracted features are subsequently mapped into the collaborative latent space to mitigate the cold-start dilemma. 

• DropoutNet [18]: Designed specifically for cold-start scenarios, this framework applies a targeted dropout mechanism to user and item identity embeddings during the training phase. This deliberate perturbation forces the model to rely on content features for preference prediction under conditions of severe interaction sparsity. 

• MetaEmbedding [19]: Operating as a meta-learning paradigm for cold-start recom-mendation, this method optimizes an embedding generator. 5.1.5 Hyperparameter Settings 

Adhering to the standard configurations established by the baseline NGCF and LightGCN models [12,13], we uniformly fix the embedding dimensionality at 𝑑 = 64 for all experiments and initialize identity embeddings via the Xavier method [42]. The training procedure follows a decoupled two-stage schedule. Specifically, the teacher network undergoes pre-training for 600 epochs, followed by a knowledge distillation phase of 200 epochs to guarantee comprehensive convergence of the model parameters at each stage.

5.2.2 Effect of Various Loss Functions 

To rigorously isolate the contributions of core components within DualGCN, we conduct extensive ablation studies across the Books, Electronics, and Sports datasets. As detailed in Ta-ble 4, all model variants maintain highly consistent evaluation metrics in warm-start scenarios. For instance, the Recall@20 metric on the Books dataset uniformly stabilizes at approximately 0.126. This consistency perfectly aligns with the design rationale of our dynamic norm re-construction mechanism, which aims to resolve the cold-start bottleneck without degrading established collaborative signals. Consequently, the subsequent analysis predominantly focuses on strict cold-start performance. During the second-stage cross-spatial targeted transfer, we evaluate our default cosine dis-tillation loss against alternative objectives, specifically Mean Squared Error and InfoNCE. Empirical results confirm that the default cosine formulation achieves the optimal comprehen-22 sive performance in cold-start settings. Conversely, the application of either Mean Squared Error or InfoNCE precipitates noticeable performance degradation. Specifically, substituting the objective with Mean Squared Error causes the Recall@20 on the Books and Electronics datasets to drop to 0.0951 and 0.0236, respectively, falling significantly short of the default scores of 0.1007 and 0.0248. The fundamental mechanism driving this phenomenon lies in the rigid constraint imposed by Mean Squared Error on absolute Euclidean distances. In graph collaborative filtering, the absolute magnitude of a node representation implicitly encodes the interaction frequency prior of the corresponding item—a prior inherently absent for cold-start items. Forcing the mapping network to approximate the absolute magnitudes of the teacher model induces magnitude distor-tion, thereby misleading the model into overfitting spurious popularity signals. In stark contrast, the cosine distillation adopted by DualGCN strips away these absolute constraints, executing correlation alignment exclusively on the unit hypersphere, which fundamentally enhances the model’s generalization capabilities. Furthermore, incorporating the InfoNCE objective similarly fails to yield performance div-idends, even precipitating a severe decline in Recall@20 to 0.0143 on the Electronics dataset. From a mechanistic perspective, the primary objective of targeted distillation in this asym-metric framework is to achieve precise coordinate projection. Indiscriminately introducing negative samples generates extraneous repulsive forces. These forces actively disrupt the deli-cate geometric alignment of local features, ultimately culminating in the observed performance deterioration. 

5.2.3 Effectiveness of Semantic Residual Design 

To further validate the architectural soundness of the student branch, we evaluate a model variant devoid of the semantic residual connection, denoted as w/o Semantic Residual . Empirical results indicate that eliminating this component precipitates a consistent and stable decline in cold-start performance across all three datasets. For instance, the Recall@20 metric drops from 0.1007 to 0.0905 on the Books dataset and from 0.1119 to 0.1010 on the Sports dataset. Given the inherent distribution gap between the static semantic space and the dynamic collab-orative manifold, compelling the network to execute a direct, deep non-linear mapping renders it highly susceptible to representation degradation and the subsequent loss of initial semantic priors. The residual mechanism effectively circumvents this vulnerability. By establishing 23 explicit semantic features as foundational structural anchors, this design empirically alleviates the optimization complexity inherent in cross-modal alignment. Consequently, it substantially enhances the fidelity of the approximate collaborative projections generated for cold-start items. 

# 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we systematically investigate the fundamental structural vulnerability of tra-ditional graph collaborative filtering models when confronted with strict cold-start scenarios. Specifically, we identify that the topological isolation of unobserved items severs the message-passing mechanism, precipitating a catastrophic collapse in representation learning. To over-come this inherent limitation, we propose DualGCN, an innovative dual-branch graph knowledge distillation framework. By seamlessly integrating high-dimensional semantic priors extracted from pre-trained language models, we fundamentally reshape the collaborative latent space. The proposed framework operates through a decoupled two-stage training paradigm. Ini-tially, the teacher branch constructs a robust, topology-smoothed collaborative reference frame anchored by semantic features. Subsequently, the student branch leverages a residual mapping network and a cosine distillation objective to achieve highly reliable cross-modal alignment. Furthermore, to unify the global retrieval space, we introduce a dynamic norm reconstruction mechanism that rigorously calibrates physical scales during inference, effectively circumventing magnitude-induced representation bias. Extensive empirical evaluations across three real-world benchmark datasets comprehen-sively validate the superiority of DualGCN. The proposed architecture not only establishes a new state-of-the-art for strict cold-start recommendation but also strictly preserves the represen-tation capacity for conventional warm-start items without incurring substantial computational overhead.
