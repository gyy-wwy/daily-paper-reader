## 摘要
生成式推荐模型通过将物品表示为语义ID并自回归地生成这些ID，已展现出良好效果。我们提出一种方法，表明将分层和属性级监督显式地融入生成式推荐，能够同时提升训练效率和长尾推荐性能。现有方法通常使用标准因果交叉熵目标来优化语义ID预测，这并未显式利用语义ID的层次结构，且可能需要大量训练才能学到有效的物品表示。此外，数据稀疏的长尾物品由于监督有限，往往表示质量较差，导致长尾推荐性能较弱。我们通过HASTE（面向生成式推荐的分层与属性感知监督）应对这些挑战，这是一个统一的训练框架，引入了分层监督和属性特定监督。HASTE的分层损失显式利用语义ID的多级结构，在物品层次的每一级提供监督，并显著加速训练收敛。其属性特定损失鼓励模型保留细粒度物品属性，利用语言模型固有的语义知识来改善长尾物品的表示与推荐。我们在一个时尚推荐基准以及亚马逊数据集的多个领域上对HASTE进行了广泛评估。HASTE在跨数据集和不同物品频次区间下均提升了推荐性能。值得注意的是，与标准因果交叉熵目标相比，分层损失实现了约2倍的收敛加速，在保持较强推荐质量的同时显著提升了训练效率。

## Abstract
Generative recommendation models have shown promising results by representing items as semantic IDs and generating these IDs autoregressively. We introduce an approach showing that explicitly incorporating hierarchical and attribute-level supervision into generative recommendation can simultaneously improve training efficiency and long-tail recommendation performance. Existing approaches typically optimize semantic ID prediction with a standard causal cross-entropy objective, which does not explicitly exploit the hierarchical structure of semantic IDs and can require substantial training to learn effective item representations. Moreover, data-sparse tail items often suffer from poor representations due to limited supervision, resulting in weaker long-tail recommendation performance. We address these challenges with HASTE (Hierarchical and Attribute-aware Supervision for generaTive rEcommendation), a unified training framework that introduces hierarchical and attribute-specific supervision. HASTE's Hierarchical Loss explicitly exploits the multi-level structure of semantic IDs, providing supervision at each level of the item hierarchy and substantially accelerating training convergence. Its Attribute-Specific Losses encourage the model to preserve fine-grained item attributes, leveraging the language model's inherent semantic knowledge to improve representations and recommendations for tail items. We extensively evaluate HASTE on a fashion recommendation benchmark and across multiple domains of the Amazon dataset. HASTE improves recommendation performance across datasets and item-frequency regimes. Notably, the Hierarchical Loss achieves approximately 2x faster convergence than the standard causal cross-entropy objective, substantially improving training efficiency while maintaining strong recommendation quality.
